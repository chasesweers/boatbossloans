Yes. Getting pre-approved before you shop tells you what you can comfortably spend, shows sellers you are a serious buyer, and lets you move quickly when you find the right boat. It also gives you a financing option to compare against anything a dealer offers.

Shopping first and figuring out financing later is how people end up emotionally attached to a boat they can't comfortably afford. Flip the order.

## What does pre-approval actually mean?

A pre-approval means a lender has reviewed your credit and financial information and indicated how much they're prepared to lend, subject to final conditions. Those conditions usually include details about the specific boat, like its value, age, and survey results, plus final verification of your documents. It is not a final loan, but it is a strong signal of where you stand.

## Why does it make shopping easier?

- **You know your budget.** Instead of guessing, you shop within a range that a lender has already looked at.
- **Sellers take you seriously.** Especially in private sales, a pre-approved buyer is more attractive than one who still has to "check with the bank."
- **You can move fast.** Good boats sell quickly. With financing lined up, you can make a confident offer.
- **You have leverage.** If a dealer offers financing, you have something real to compare it against. See [Should I finance through the dealer or on my own?](/guide/dealer-vs-own-financing).

## Does pre-approval cost anything or hurt my credit?

Applying typically involves a credit inquiry. Scoring models generally treat several inquiries for the same kind of loan within a short window as a single event, so a focused shopping period is usually less of a concern than people expect. Ask your lending specialist how they pull credit before you apply. Pre-approval itself doesn't commit you to anything.

## How long is a pre-approval good for?

That varies by lender. Pre-approvals usually come with an expiration, because your credit and the market can change. If your search takes longer than expected, your lending specialist can update it. Try to get pre-approved when you are ready to shop seriously, not a year ahead.

## What do I need to get pre-approved?

Have these ready to make it quick:

- Basic personal and contact information
- Employment and income details. Self-employed buyers should have recent tax returns handy
- A rough idea of the kind of boat and price range you're considering
- Information on your existing monthly obligations, like your mortgage and car loans

You don't need to have picked out a specific boat. That comes later, when the lender reviews the boat itself before final approval.

## What happens after I find the boat?

Once you have a signed purchase agreement, your lending specialist collects the boat details, such as the hull ID, engine information, and title or documentation, and orders anything else needed, like a survey or proof of insurance. When the conditions are met, the loan moves to closing. We walk through the whole process in [How do boat loans work?](/guide/how-boat-loans-work).

## Is pre-approval worth it if I'm paying mostly cash?

Even cash-heavy buyers often find it useful. Financing part of the purchase can keep cash available for the things that come with owning a boat: insurance, storage, maintenance, and gear. Talk through the trade-offs with your lending specialist and your own financial advisor.
