Yes. Used boats are financed every day, from dealers and from private sellers. Lenders look closely at the boat's age, condition, and value, so a well-documented boat with a clean survey is the easiest kind to finance.

A pre-owned boat can be a smart way to get more boat for your money. Here is what changes when you finance one.

## How do lenders decide what a used boat is worth?

The boat is the collateral, so its value matters. Lenders use industry pricing guides and recent comparable sales to estimate what a boat is worth, and they may rely on a marine survey for larger or older boats. If the price you agreed to is well above what the guides and comparables support, the lender may finance less than you hoped, and you'd need to cover the difference in cash.

## Does the age of the boat matter?

Yes. Each lender has its own guidelines about how old a boat it will finance and how age affects the deal. Newer boats are generally the simplest. Older boats can still be financed, especially well-known models that hold their value, but the lender may ask for more information. We go deeper in [How old a boat can I finance?](/guide/how-old-a-boat-can-i-finance), and because those guidelines vary, your lending specialist is the right person to give you specifics.

## What about engine hours and condition?

Hours and maintenance history tell a big part of a used boat's story. A boat with high hours and a stack of service records may be a better bet than a low-hour boat that sat neglected. Lenders care because condition affects value; you should care because it affects what you'll spend keeping the boat running. Ask the seller for:

- Service and maintenance records
- Engine hours, and the history of any major repairs or repowers
- Records of any accident or insurance claims
- The title or documentation history

## Will I need a marine survey?

For many used boats, especially larger or older ones, lenders and insurers will ask for a survey. Even when it isn't required, it's the best protection you have against expensive surprises. Read [Do I need a marine survey to get a loan?](/guide/marine-survey-for-boat-loan) for what a surveyor checks.

## Can I include the trailer or new electronics?

Often, yes. Many buyers roll a trailer, upgraded electronics, or other equipment into the purchase. What can be included depends on the lender and how the deal is structured. See [Can I include engines, electronics, or a trailer in the loan?](/guide/finance-engines-electronics-trailer).

## Dealer or private seller: does it change financing?

Both work. Dealers handle the title and paperwork for you; private sales need a little more coordination, like confirming the seller owns the boat outright and paying off any existing lien at closing. Our guide to [financing a boat from a private seller](/guide/private-party-boat-loan) covers the steps.

## What should I do before making an offer?

1. **Get pre-approved,** so you know your budget and can move quickly on the right boat.
2. **Check value** against pricing guides and comparable listings.
3. **Schedule a survey and sea trial,** and make your offer contingent on both.
4. **Get an insurance quote** early. Insurance on some older or specialized boats can take time to arrange.
5. **Confirm the paperwork:** title or documentation, hull ID, engine serials, and lien status.
