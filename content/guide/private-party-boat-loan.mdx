Yes, many marine lenders finance private-party boat purchases, not just dealer sales. The difference is that you and the lender take on the paperwork a dealer would normally handle, like verifying the title, clearing any existing liens, and documenting the boat's condition and value.

Some of the best boats on the market are sold by owners, not dealers. Financing one is very doable. It just takes a little more care.

## How is a private sale different from buying from a dealer?

At a dealership, the F&I office handles the title work, payoff of any existing loan, registration, and paperwork. In a private sale, nobody is doing that for you by default. The lender needs to be sure that the seller actually owns the boat, that any existing loan gets paid off at closing, and that the lender's lien is recorded properly. A lender or closing service experienced with private-party deals coordinates these steps.

## What does the lender need to see?

Expect a lender to ask for some combination of:

- **Purchase agreement** signed by you and the seller, listing the price, the boat, engines, and trailer if included.
- **Title or documentation.** For titled boats, a copy of the title. For federally documented vessels, the Coast Guard documentation.
- **Hull identification number** and engine serial numbers, so everything matches the paperwork.
- **Lien information.** If the seller still owes money, the lender needs the payoff details so it can be cleared at closing.
- **Proof of value and condition.** Often a marine survey, especially on larger or older boats.
- **Insurance** naming the lender before funds are released.

## Who pays off the seller's existing loan?

This is the part that worries people most, and it shouldn't be done on a handshake. In a properly structured private-party closing, funds are distributed so that the seller's existing lender is paid directly and releases its lien, and the remaining balance goes to the seller. You should never hand money to a seller who still owes on the boat and simply trust that they will pay it off.

## Do I need a marine survey?

Often, yes. Lenders frequently require a survey for private-party purchases, particularly on larger or older boats, and even when it isn't required it is money well spent. A survey tells you, and the lender, what you are really buying. See [Do I need a marine survey to get a loan?](/guide/marine-survey-for-boat-loan) for the details.

## How do I protect myself from scams?

Private sales attract the occasional bad actor. Protect yourself:

1. **Verify ownership.** The name on the title or documentation should match the seller's ID.
2. **Match the numbers.** Check the hull ID and engine serials against the paperwork yourself.
3. **Run a lien search.** Your lender or closing service will usually do this.
4. **See the boat in person,** ideally in the water and with a sea trial.
5. **Never wire money directly to a seller** based on emailed instructions. Let the lender or closing agent handle the funds.
