Often, yes: many buyers with bumps in their credit history still finance a boat, though the options and terms may differ. What matters most is the full picture, including why your credit took a hit, what you have done since, and the deal you are putting together.

Life happens. Medical bills, a divorce, a business that hit a rough patch. A lender's job is to understand the risk, and your job is to help them see it clearly.

## What counts as "less-than-perfect" credit?

There is no official definition. It can mean a few late payments, high card balances, a thin credit file with little history, a past collection account, or something more serious like a bankruptcy or repossession. Each of these is viewed differently, and how long ago it happened matters a great deal. A problem from years back, followed by a clean record, tells a very different story than a recent one.

## Do some lenders specialize in this?

Lenders have different appetites. Some focus on borrowers with very strong credit; others are set up to work with a broader range of profiles. ## What can strengthen my application?

You can't rewrite your history, but you can make the deal around it stronger:

- **A larger cash investment.** Putting more of your own money into the purchase lowers the lender's risk. How much helps depends on the lender and the deal, so ask your lending specialist.
- **A co-borrower.** A spouse or partner with solid credit and income can change the outcome. Both of you share responsibility for the loan, so go in with eyes open.
- **The right boat.** A boat with strong resale value and a clean survey is easier to finance than one that is hard to value. Sometimes stepping down in size or age is the difference.
- **Documented income.** Clear, verifiable income reassures lenders. Self-employed buyers should have tax returns and business statements ready.
- **A written explanation.** If a specific event caused your credit problems, a short, honest letter explaining what happened and what has changed can help.

## What should I expect to be different?

With a weaker profile, lenders may offer different terms than they would to a borrower with excellent credit, ask for more documentation, or want a larger cash investment. That is normal. Your lending specialist should explain exactly what is being offered and why. Never sign anything you don't understand, and ask how the terms affect your total cost over the life of the loan, not just the payment.

## Should I fix my credit first or apply now?

It depends on your timeline and how far off you are. If a few months of paying down cards or clearing up an error would make a meaningful difference, waiting can pay off. If your credit is already on the mend and you have found the right boat, it may make sense to apply and see where you stand. A lending specialist can tell you which path makes sense, and that conversation is a lot more useful than guessing.

## Watch out for promises that sound too good

Be wary of anyone who promises approval before they've reviewed your credit, or who pressures you to sign quickly. No legitimate lender can promise approval sight unseen. Take your time, read the paperwork, and ask questions until the answers make sense.
